# Per-page review: Enterprise Agentic AI Adoption Compass 2026

24 reviewer agents, one per page (violet edition, v17, 2026-08-10). Scores: Fortune-500 readiness out of 10.

## Page 1 · score 6/10
**Strengths:**
- Dek is thesis-led, not boilerplate: 'Nine layers... Two of them set the pace' states a falsifiable claim and a promise (the sequence across the gap) in three sentences — rare discipline for a cover.
- WHY NOW block follows the right consulting convention: letterspaced label over a hairline rule, one bolded, dated, attributed stat (17% deployed / 42% plan, Gartner 2026) instead of vague urgency.
- Typographic system is confident and print-ready: serif display title with muted-violet '2026' accent, sans utility text, monochrome one-line logo strip — coherent premium hierarchy on the dark ground.
- The bottom-right heatmap bleeding off the page edge is a genuine teaser of the report's core exhibit (levels x layers) rather than decorative stock art.
**Weaknesses:**
- The McKinsey disclaimer is self-undermining: naming McKinsey & Company on your own cover concedes the design imitates their trade dress and invites the comparison; it is also set at roughly 6pt — barely legible in A4 print.
- Copy defects in the highest-visibility paragraph: '42 percent plan to within twelve months' is clipped (plan to deploy), and 'This compass is the map through the trough' mixes the compass/map metaphor in the report's own name sentence.
- Teaser graphic is half-labeled and internally inconsistent: x-axis reads Level 0/2/4/6 for a 7-level scale yet the grid shows 8+ columns, the nine rows carry no label or caption, and the highlighted cells have no key — it reads decorative, and slightly wrong, to a careful reader.
- Composition has a hollow center: a full-width dead band from roughly 40% to 60% of page height between the WHY NOW block and the authors/graphic leaves the page top-left-heavy and bottom-heavy with nothing anchoring the middle or upper right.
**Missing:**
- No issuing entity or publisher anywhere — only two author names plus borrowed university logos ('research affiliations of the authors'); a Fortune 500 reader will ask who stands behind and maintains this report, and the cover does not answer.
- No precise publication date or edition marker — '2026' alone; a compass claiming currency ('capability compounds every quarter') needs at least month + version to be citable and shelvable.
- No contents cue or payoff pointer: the cover promises 'the sequence that crosses the gap' and that two layers set the pace, but offers no what's-inside strip, section list, or page pointer to guide an exec into the 24 pages.
